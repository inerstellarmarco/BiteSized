## Items, bills of materials, suppliers, and stock levels
Items, bills of materials, suppliers, and stock levels are the foundational master records that describe what a business makes, what parts it buys, who sells them, and how much sits on the shelf. Every planning calculation depends on these four records being accurate.

Think of a professional kitchen that keeps a master ingredient list, recipe cards, approved food wholesalers, and a daily pantry count so the head chef knows exactly what can be cooked tonight.

The caveat is that messy master data cripples any AI assistant you build on top. Before connecting an agent, check whether your client has duplicate part numbers or outdated supplier records.

?? Why are clean item, supplier, and stock records essential before adding AI to an operations system?
+ Every downstream planning calculation and agent action relies on those master records being accurate
- Language models cannot read database tables unless part numbers are written in words
- Suppliers refuse to ship goods to companies that use automated inventory software
- Clean master records remove the need to count physical stock in the warehouse
= Master records form the foundation of ERP and MRP systems, so duplicates or errors distort every plan and recommendation.

## Bill of materials: the parts list for a product, including sub-assemblies
A bill of materials, or BOM, is the structured recipe that lists every raw material, component, sub-assembly, and quantity required to build one finished unit of a product. Multi-level bills of materials nest smaller assemblies inside larger ones.

For example, a bicycle manufacturer's BOM for a touring bike lists one frame assembly, two wheel assemblies, and one handlebar kit, where each wheel assembly has its own list of spokes, rims, and tyres.

A common pitfall is when engineering updates a product design but forgets to update the BOM in the system. When that happens, purchasing buys the wrong parts no matter how clever your software is.

?? What does a multi-level bill of materials describe in a manufacturing business?
+ The exact hierarchy of raw materials, components, and sub-assemblies needed to build one finished product
- The marketing copy and product photographs used in the annual sales catalogue
- The list of customers who have overdue invoices from the previous quarter
- The personal contact details and shift preferences of warehouse employees
= A bill of materials defines the exact parts and nested sub-assemblies required to produce a finished item.

## Sales orders, work orders, and purchase orders
Sales orders, work orders, and purchase orders are the three core transaction documents that move goods through a business: customers place sales orders, the factory runs work orders to build items, and buyers send purchase orders to external suppliers.

It is like a bespoke tailor who writes down a customer's suit order, issues a cutting ticket to the workshop sewists, and sends a fabric order to the wool mill.

Keep these three document types clearly separated when designing AI workflows. Confusing an internal work order with an external purchase order can cause an agent to email an internal production note to an outside vendor.

?? How do sales orders, work orders, and purchase orders differ in an ERP system?
+ Sales orders record customer demand, work orders instruct internal production, and purchase orders buy from outside suppliers
- All three documents are identical invoices sent to the tax authority at year end
- Purchase orders are used inside the factory while work orders are sent to customers
- Sales orders only track raw materials that have failed quality inspection
= Each order type governs a distinct stage: selling to customers, making goods internally, and buying inputs from suppliers.

## Routings: the steps and work centres used to make a product
A routing defines the step-by-step sequence of operations, machines, and work centres required to manufacture a product, along with the setup and run time expected at each stage. While the bill of materials says what goes into the product, the routing says how it is made.

For instance, making a metal bracket might route the steel sheet through laser cutting at Work Centre 1, bending at Work Centre 2, and powder coating at Work Centre 3.

The limit is that standard routing times on paper often drift from real shop-floor speeds. Consultants can add real value by analysing actual completion logs against routing estimates to spot hidden bottlenecks.

?? While a bill of materials lists the parts needed for a product, what does a routing specify?
+ The sequence of operations, work centres, and expected times required to manufacture the item
- The delivery truck GPS route used by the courier after the item is sold
- The bank account numbers used to pay international raw material brokers
- The office seating chart for the company's human resources department
= Routings capture the manufacturing steps, work centres, and labour or machine hours needed to turn parts into finished goods.

## Lead times: how long suppliers and production take
Lead time is the total calendar time that passes from placing a purchase or work order until the items are received, inspected, and ready to use in stock. It dictates how far in advance a planner must act before stock runs out.

It is like ordering a custom wedding cake; if the bakery needs three weeks notice, ordering two days before the ceremony leaves you empty-handed regardless of your budget.

Never assume supplier lead times are static constants. Shipping delays, seasonal peaks, and factory shutdowns make lead times vary, so static ERP fields often need periodic updating from actual delivery history.

?? Why do inaccurate lead times in an ERP system cause serious inventory problems?
+ The system will trigger purchase and production orders either too late, causing stockouts, or far too early
- Inaccurate lead times prevent the accounting software from adding sales tax to invoices
- Suppliers automatically cancel contracts if a buyer's internal lead time field is wrong
- Lead times only affect digital downloads that have no physical shipping steps
= Material planning relies on lead times to schedule orders at the right moment before stock is needed.

## Unit costs and how they roll up
Unit cost roll-up is the calculation that adds the cost of raw materials, bought-in components, direct labour, and machine overhead through every level of the bill of materials to find the true cost of one finished product.

Think of pricing a restaurant dish by summing the cost of beef, pastry, mushrooms, and prep kitchen labour to know whether selling the plate at £28 actually leaves a profit margin.

The caveat is that when raw material prices jump, top-level product margins quietly shrink unless costs are rolled up again. AI agents can help finance teams spot products whose rolled-up cost has crept too close to their selling price.

?? What happens during a unit cost roll-up across a multi-level bill of materials?
+ Material, sub-assembly, labour, and overhead costs are summed up the hierarchy to calculate the finished item's cost
- Every item in the warehouse is assigned the exact same average selling price
- Supplier invoices are deleted once the goods pass incoming quality inspection
- Customer shipping fees are subtracted from employee payroll taxes
= Rolling up costs aggregates every input and processing step so management knows the true cost of producing each unit.

## Material requirements planning: what to buy, what to make, and when
Material requirements planning, or MRP, is the deterministic calculation engine that works backwards from customer delivery dates and demand forecasts to schedule exactly which parts to buy, which assemblies to make, and when to launch each order.

It is like planning a large family roast dinner by starting from the 2:00 pm serving time and calculating when the turkey, potatoes, and yorkshire puddings must each go into the oven.

Remember that standard MRP assumes infinite factory capacity unless paired with capacity scheduling. If your client's machines are already running flat out, an MRP run may schedule more work for Tuesday than the workshop can physically complete.

?? How does a material requirements planning (MRP) engine calculate order schedules?
+ It works backwards from required delivery dates using bills of materials, stock levels, and lead times
- It asks a language model to guess random order quantities each morning
- It waits until every shelf in the warehouse is completely empty before ordering parts
- It copies last year's purchase orders without checking current customer demand
= MRP backward-schedules production and purchasing from target dates so materials arrive just in time for each step.

## Inputs: demand, stock, open orders, bills of materials, and lead times
Every MRP run relies on five strict inputs: gross demand from sales and forecasts, current stock on hand, open purchase and work orders already in flight, bills of materials, and item lead times. Missing any one input breaks the calculation.

Imagine planning a household grocery shop; to avoid buying six extra cartons of milk, you must know what meals are planned, what is already in the fridge, and whether your partner already ordered groceries online.

For an AI consultant, this means an agent cannot answer "how many valves should we buy today" by looking at sales orders alone; it must query all five inputs together from the ERP.

?? What happens if a planning calculation ignores open purchase orders that are already on their way?
+ The business will order duplicate stock that is already scheduled to arrive
- The warehouse stock count for finished goods will automatically drop to zero
- Customers will be blocked from placing new orders on the company website
- The bill of materials will permanently delete its sub-assembly links
= Ignoring open orders causes the planner to buy materials twice because it fails to count stock already in transit.

## Reorder points and safety stock
A reorder point is the stock threshold that triggers a replenishment order, while safety stock is the extra buffer held above expected lead-time demand to protect against unexpected sales spikes or late supplier deliveries.

It is like keeping a spare jerrycan of petrol in the garage and refilling your car's tank whenever the gauge hits one quarter full, rather than waiting for the engine to splutter on the motorway.

The trade-off is that safety stock ties up cash and warehouse space. Holding massive buffers hides every operational problem at the expense of working capital, whereas holding zero buffer leaves the factory fragile to the slightest delay.

?? What is the purpose of holding safety stock in an inventory system?
+ To buffer the business against unexpected demand spikes or supplier delivery delays during the lead time
- To fill empty warehouse shelves so the building looks busy during visitor tours
- To avoid ever having to record purchase orders in the accounting system
- To guarantee that slow-moving items expire before they can be sold
= Safety stock absorbs variability in supply and demand so minor disruptions do not immediately halt production or sales.

## Lot sizes and minimum order quantities
Lot sizing rules and minimum order quantities (MOQs) govern the batch increments in which items must be bought or manufactured. Even if a factory only needs twelve custom screws this week, a supplier may enforce a minimum order of one thousand.

Think of buying hamburger buns at a supermarket where rolls come in packs of eight; if you are hosting ten guests, you must buy two packs of eight rather than ten individual buns.

When building procurement agents, ensure the system respects supplier MOQs and packaging multiples. Suggesting a purchase order for 43 circuit boards when the vendor only ships in boxes of 50 will cause the supplier to reject the order.

?? Why must an automated purchasing workflow check minimum order quantities and pack multiples?
+ Suppliers will reject or alter orders that do not match their required batch sizes and minimums
- Ordering in exact pack multiples makes cargo ships travel twice as fast
- Minimum order quantities only apply to office stationery and never to raw materials
- Lot sizes determine the legal tax rate applied to employee wages
= Real-world suppliers and production lines operate in fixed minimums and multiples that every valid order must respect.

## Demand forecasts feed the plan
Demand forecasts estimate future customer orders over the coming weeks or months so the business can buy long-lead-time materials before firm sales orders arrive. Whenever supplier lead times are longer than the time customers are willing to wait, forecasting is unavoidable.

For instance, a garden furniture maker must order aluminium tubing in January based on spring sales forecasts, because waiting until sunny weekends in May would mean missing the entire season.

Keep in mind that every forecast is wrong to some degree. Good planning systems measure forecast error regularly and adjust safety stock accordingly, rather than treating an AI forecast as a guaranteed promise.

?? Why must a manufacturer rely on demand forecasts when ordering long-lead-time components?
+ Customers will not wait months for delivery, so parts must be ordered before firm sales orders arrive
- Forecasts are legally required to replace actual sales orders in the company ledger
- Forecasting guarantees that customer demand will never fluctuate from week to week
- Suppliers give a 90 percent discount whenever an order is based on a forecast
= When cumulative procurement and build times exceed the customer's delivery window, materials must be ordered ahead of confirmed sales.

## Garbage in, garbage out: wrong stock counts or lead times ruin the plan
Garbage in, garbage out means that even the most mathematically sound MRP engine or intelligent agent will produce disastrous purchase plans if the underlying stock counts, bills of materials, or lead times are wrong.

It is like using a satellite navigation app when the map database has the wrong bridge closures; the software calculates a route with total confidence and drives you straight into a dead end.

If a warehouse shelf says 500 bearings are in stock when there are really only 50, the system will refuse to reorder and the production line will grind to a halt. Always audit data accuracy alongside software setup.

?? What happens when an ERP system has phantom inventory showing 500 units on hand when only 50 physically exist?
+ The planning engine assumes stock is plenty and fails to reorder in time, causing a production stop
- The software automatically conducts a physical warehouse count using security cameras
- The supplier sends 450 free units without waiting for a purchase order
- The unit cost of the remaining 50 units drops to zero in the accounting ledger
= Planning logic trusts the database figures, so overstated stock levels prevent timely reordering and lead directly to shortages.

## ERP ties inventory, purchasing, sales, and accounting together
Enterprise resource planning, or ERP, is an integrated software system where sales, purchasing, warehouse inventory, production, and financial accounting share a single database. When one department records an event, every connected department sees the update immediately.

Think of the nervous system in a human body; when your hand touches a hot kettle, your eyes, muscles, and brain coordinate instantly rather than sending letters to each other by post.

Because everything connects, a careless change in one module ripples everywhere. If an AI agent alters an item's unit of measure from boxes to individual pieces in inventory, it can accidentally multiply invoice values across accounting.

?? Why must an AI consultant be cautious when letting an agent modify records inside an ERP system?
+ Because inventory, purchasing, sales, and accounting share the same data, one bad edit ripples across departments
- ERP systems delete their entire database whenever an external API logs in
- Accounting modules can only be updated once a year on the final day of the tax period
- Changing an inventory record only affects the warehouse and never touches financial numbers
= In an integrated ERP, operational transactions flow directly into purchasing commitments and financial ledgers.

## One system of record prevents conflicting numbers
A single system of record is the authoritative database that the entire company agrees holds the true figure for stock, orders, and balances. Without it, departments waste hours arguing over whose spreadsheet is right in Monday morning meetings.

It is like having one official scoreboard at a football stadium; the match becomes chaos if the referee, the home fans, and the away bench each keep a different score on paper.

When you build AI tools for a client, never let the agent create a separate shadow database that drifts out of sync with the ERP. Always read from and write back to the true system of record.

?? What problem does a single system of record solve for a growing business?
+ It prevents departments from working off conflicting spreadsheets and arguing over which numbers are true
- It guarantees that customers will always pay their invoices ahead of the due date
- It removes the need to set user permissions or keep database backups
- It allows every employee to edit financial statements without an audit trail
= Having one authoritative source of truth ensures sales, operations, and finance act on the exact same figures.

## Small companies often start with spreadsheets and grow into ERP
Most small manufacturers and distributors begin by tracking stock and orders in spreadsheets because spreadsheets are cheap, flexible, and familiar. As order volume and team size grow, manual copy-pasting and broken formulas eventually force a move to structured ERP software.

It is like running a market stall out of a shoebox of cash receipts; it works fine for twenty sales a Saturday, but collapses once you open three high-street shops with twelve staff.

As a consultant, recognise where your client sits on this curve. Sometimes a ten-person firm needs a simple structured database first rather than a million-pound enterprise ERP implementation.

?? Why do growing companies eventually outgrow running their core operations on spreadsheets?
+ Manual copy-pasting, broken formulas, and concurrent edits lead to costly errors as transaction volume rises
- Spreadsheet software stops opening once a company hires its tenth employee
- Tax authorities forbid businesses from exporting financial reports to spreadsheet files
- Spreadsheets cannot perform basic addition or multiplication on inventory counts
= While spreadsheets are flexible early on, they lack the relational integrity, audit trails, and concurrency controls needed at scale.

## Where AI helps most: data entry, exceptions, and answering questions
In ERP and MRP environments, AI delivers the highest return in three practical areas: extracting data from messy incoming documents, flagging operational exceptions that need human attention, and answering plain-language questions over structured records.

Think of an AI assistant as a sharp junior clerk who reads the morning post, highlights the three urgent supplier problems in yellow, and fetches files from the cabinet whenever the manager asks, while leaving core accounting math to the calculator.

Do not use language models to replace the deterministic MRP calculation engine itself. Let standard database code calculate stock balances and requirements, and use AI around the edges where unstructured text and human judgement meet.

?? Which tasks are best suited for AI inside an ERP and MRP environment?
+ Reading unstructured documents, flagging operational exceptions, and answering plain-language data questions
- Replacing deterministic database arithmetic with approximate language model guesses
- Deleting audit trails so the database uses less storage space each month
- Approving large bank transfers without checking purchase orders or receipts
= AI excels at bridging unstructured documents and human questions, while deterministic code handles exact calculations and ledger entries.

Neither path wins every time. Wrapping an existing ERP preserves years of accounting compliance and historical data, while a lightweight custom system works well when a small business has no ERP yet or only needs a narrow operational tool.

?? What is the main architectural trade-off between adding agents to an existing ERP and building a lightweight custom system?
+ Keeping the existing ERP preserves proven accounting and history, while a custom build offers flexibility at the cost of owning every feature

## Data modelling basics: tables, keys, and relationships
Relational data modelling organises business records into separate tables for items, suppliers, orders, and order lines, linked together by unique primary keys and foreign keys. This structure ensures every piece of information is stored once and referenced reliably.

Think of a library catalogue where each author has a unique ID card and each book record points to that author ID, so changing an author's biography updates every book entry automatically.

Never store a purchase order with ten items by cramming all ten part numbers into a single comma-separated text box. Keep an orders header table and a linked order lines table so queries and agents can sum quantities cleanly.

?? Why should an order management database separate order headers and order line items into linked tables?
+ It allows each line item to be queried, summed, and validated cleanly using primary and foreign keys
- It makes the database impossible for human accountants to read
- Storing all items inside a single comma-separated text cell is faster to sum in SQL
- Separate tables remove the need for unique item part numbers
= Proper relational tables with keys prevent data duplication and allow exact filtering and aggregation across orders and items.

## Calculations run in code; the LLM orchestrates and explains
In a reliable operations architecture, deterministic code and database queries execute every arithmetic calculation and planning rule, while the language model orchestrates tool calls and explains the results in plain English.

It is like a courtroom where the forensic accountant calculates the exact tax figures on a spreadsheet, and the barrister presents those verified numbers clearly to the jury.

If a user asks for the total value of late purchase orders, the LLM should invoke a verified SQL tool or API endpoint rather than trying to add seventy invoice lines inside its own context window.

?? In an AI-assisted ERP workflow, what should perform the arithmetic and what should the language model do?
+ Deterministic code runs the arithmetic and rules, while the language model calls tools and explains the results
- The language model calculates all financial totals in its prompt while code only changes screen colours
- Both the database and the language model guess the totals and average their two answers
- Neither system performs calculations because users must add invoice lines on pocket calculators
= Separating exact code calculation from natural-language orchestration prevents arithmetic hallucinations in business data.

## Why: models slip on arithmetic and rules; code does not
Language models predict the next likely token based on patterns rather than executing exact mathematical logic, which means they periodically fumble multi-step arithmetic, rounding rules, and date calculations. Standard code executes the same formula identically a million times in a row.

Imagine asking a talented poet to multiply four-digit numbers in their head during a noisy dinner party; they might get several right, but you would never bet the company payroll on their mental sums.

Even when a frontier model gets 98 percent of test sums right, a 2 percent error rate in inventory balances or VAT calculations will corrupt an ERP ledger within days.

?? Why is a 98 percent mental arithmetic accuracy rate from a language model unacceptable for core ERP calculations?
+ Even a tiny percentage of arithmetic slips will quickly corrupt stock balances, tax figures, and financial ledgers
- ERP databases reject any number that takes less than five seconds to calculate
- Language models only make arithmetic mistakes on leap years
- Standard SQL code has a lower accuracy rate than unassisted token prediction
= Core operational and financial records require 100 percent deterministic consistency, which only code and database engines provide.

## Audit trail for every change an agent makes
An audit trail is an immutable log that records every database insert, update, or status change made by a user or an AI agent, capturing who or what made the change, the timestamp, the old value, and the new value.

It is like the flight data recorder in an aircraft cockpit, preserving the exact sequence of instrument inputs and autopilot actions so investigators can reconstruct any incident step by step.

Never let an AI agent share a generic login with a human manager. Give every agent its own distinct service identity in the audit log so staff can immediately filter which records were touched by automation.

?? Why should every AI agent have its own distinct identity in the ERP audit log rather than sharing a human user's login?
+ So administrators can see exactly which changes were made by the agent, when they happened, and what the previous values were
- Because database servers run twice as fast when usernames contain the word agent
- So the human manager can avoid paying income tax on their salary
- Because audit logs only record changes made on weekends
= A separate agent identity alongside before-and-after values ensures full accountability and fast troubleshooting when an automated edit goes wrong.

## Permissions: who and what can change which records
Role-based permissions restrict each human user and AI agent to the minimum set of tables and actions required for their specific job. An agent that drafts warehouse pick lists has no business reading employee salaries or editing bank details.

Think of hotel keycards; a guest's card opens their bedroom and the gym door, a cleaner's card opens the linen cupboards on Floor 3, and neither card opens the manager's cash safe.

Enforce permissions inside the database or API layer itself, not inside the system prompt. Telling a model in a prompt "please do not update the supplier bank account table" is a polite wish, not a security control.

?? Where must access permissions for an AI agent be enforced to protect an ERP system?
+ At the API or database layer using strict role-based credentials, not merely inside the text of the prompt
- Only inside the system prompt by asking the model politely not to touch financial tables
- By hiding the computer monitor when the agent runs at night
- Permissions are unnecessary if the agent only runs inside a private company network
= Prompt instructions can be bypassed or ignored, whereas database and API credentials enforce hard boundaries on what an agent can read or write.

## Backups and a way to undo changes
Automated backups and transaction rollback procedures ensure that if an integration bug or rogue agent corrupts records, the business can restore clean data quickly without losing days of operations.

It is like a carpenter clamping a scrap piece of wood for a test cut and keeping the original timber safely stacked until the joint fits, while also having an undo button on a CNC router.

Before deploying any agent with write access, test how you would reverse fifty bad updates made in ten seconds. Combining regular database snapshots with an application-level undo script for agent batches turns a potential crisis into a five-minute recovery.

?? Why should an AI consultant build a batch-undo mechanism alongside standard database backups when giving an agent write access?
+ It lets administrators cleanly reverse a specific batch of bad agent updates without wiping out valid human work done at the same time
- It allows the company to cancel paid supplier invoices after the goods have already been sold
- Database backups alone automatically prevent agents from ever writing invalid text
- Undo scripts eliminate the need to test integrations in a sandbox environment
= While full backups protect against total disaster, targeted rollback logs let you undo faulty agent changes without losing concurrent human transactions.

## APIs, webhooks, and database access
APIs, webhooks, and direct database connections are the three technical pipes used to connect external software and AI agents to an ERP. An API lets your agent request or submit records on demand, a webhook pushes an alert when an event occurs, and direct database access queries tables straight from the server.

Think of an API as phoning the warehouse desk to ask a question, a webhook as the warehouse ringing a bell whenever a lorry arrives, and direct database access as walking into the filing room yourself.

Always prefer the ERP's official API over writing directly to its underlying SQL tables. Writing straight to the database often bypasses business validation rules and corrupts internal indexes.

?? Why is writing records through an ERP's official API safer than inserting rows directly into its underlying database tables?
+ The official API enforces the ERP's built-in validation rules, accounting links, and audit triggers
- Direct database queries travel through the internet without using electricity
- Official APIs never require authentication tokens or user permissions
- Writing directly to SQL tables is the only way to trigger a webhook notification
= ERP APIs run essential validation and bookkeeping logic that raw SQL inserts bypass, protecting ledger integrity.

## MCP servers that expose ERP data to agents
~m
A Model Context Protocol (MCP) server is a standardised wrapper that exposes selected ERP tools, queries, and resources to an AI agent through a consistent interface. Because protocol specifications and SDKs evolve rapidly, check the current details when designing your integration.

It acts like a universal travel adapter plug that lets any modern appliance connect safely to a wall socket without rewiring the building's electrical panel for each device.

When building an MCP server for a client's ERP, expose narrow, business-safe tools such as `get_late_purchase_orders` or `create_draft_po` rather than exposing a raw SQL execution tool that lets the model run arbitrary commands.

?? What is the safest way to design an MCP server that connects an AI agent to a client's ERP?
+ Expose narrow, well-validated business tools with strict parameters rather than a raw unrestricted SQL tool
- Give the MCP server root database credentials and let the model write arbitrary DELETE statements
- Expose every table including payroll and passwords so you never need to update the server later
- Skip input validation inside the MCP server because the language model will check its own parameters
